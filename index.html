A note about Netta
I’m a design leader with more than 20 years of experience building products, leading teams, and growing organizations.

Along the way I’ve been lucky enough to work with companies like Airbnb, Etsy, and Square. I later stepped into the founder seat with Domingo, where I discovered what it means to build not just a product, but an entire business.

Whether I’m leading a team, writing software, or making skincare, the work has always been driven by curiosity. And it began early.

As a kid, that meant carrying around a sketchbook and trying to recreate anything that caught my eye. Over time, drawing turned into designing interfaces, leading teams, starting companies, formulating skincare, and, eventually, building software.

Looking back, the medium has changed far more than the motivation. I’ve always been happiest making thoughtful things that help people in some way.

Founding Domingo taught me that the work I love isn’t tied to a particular industry. It’s the process of building something from nothing, learning relentlessly, and caring deeply about the people on the other side.

Building Matta reminded me just how much I enjoy making software. More importantly, it reinforced something that’s been true all along: I’m happiest when I’m learning, making, and sharing what I discover along the way.

    I've always liked making things.

    Some became companies. Some became apps. Some became websites. Some were experiments that quietly taught me something. They all represent a different season of my life, and I'm grateful for every one of them.

    Domingo is the skincare company I founded in 2023.

    What started as a personal ritual during one of the hardest seasons of my life eventually became a business focused on helping people feel safe and beautiful in their skin. Today I formulate every product, run the business, write every email, build the website, design the packaging, photograph the products, and still spend my weekends making body butter by hand.

    Building Domingo taught me what it really means to build a company, not just a product.

    www.domingo.nyc

    Matta is the project I'm currently most excited about.

    It's an AI companion designed to help people make sense of everything competing for their attention. Rather than becoming another task manager, I'm exploring what it looks like to build software that understands context, notices patterns, and helps people feel a little less overwhelmed.

    It's also been my excuse to dive back into building software, which I've discovered I still absolutely love.

    This website exists because Squarespace got too expensive.

    Rather than moving to another template, I decided to build something that felt like wandering through someone's old computer. It's heavily inspired by Windows 98 and the early web, but built to work responsively across desktop and mobile.

    It's probably the most fun I've had building a personal website and hiding little easter eggs for you.

    Hindsight was a project I created after realizing I was answering the same career questions over and over from designers just starting out.

    Instead of keeping those conversations in email, I turned them into a small website filled with the advice I wish someone had given me earlier in my career.

    The site is still online, but time hasn't been especially kind to it. I've intentionally left it untouched as a snapshot of where I was at the time rather than rebuilding it.

    Three was an iPhone weather app I designed many years ago.

    It eventually found its way onto the App Store, received recognition twice from Apple, and taught me an enormous amount about designing and shipping software.

    The app has long since been retired, but it'll always have a special place in my heart.

    Not everything I build needs to become a product.

    Lately I've been spending time following whatever sparks my curiosity next.
